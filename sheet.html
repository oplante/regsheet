Fill in your own sheet

Type the details the draft regulations list for a landlord entry and a dwelling entry. When you print, empty answers stay empty and are marked “you still need this”.

Everything stays in this browser. There is no account, no upload, and no check of your answers. A gas safety record, EICR, EIC, EPC, or authority document is not attached here, even where the draft regulations ask for a copy. Checkout is not connected, so this page does not take the £29 or £49.

Before the fields

This region line is a RegSheet helper so the printout can show the dates. It is not one of the numbered items in Schedule 2 or Schedule 3. LandlordZONE says the deadline follows the property’s location, not where the landlord lives. If these dwellings are in different regions, print a separate sheet. This form assumes one region.

Landlord entry

Schedule 2 of the draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026. One landlord entry on this sheet. The NRLA says each joint landlord needs their own landlord entry, with one dwelling entry linked to each. If that is your situation, fill and print once per landlord. This page does not decide whether you are a joint landlord.

Leave this blank if there is no Companies House number. If it is blank, the next box is one the draft regulations ask for.

The draft regulations say “if applicable”. A blank charity number is not marked missing when you print. A blank is not the same as a statement that you have no charity number.

Lead trustee

The draft regulations ask for these where the organisation is constituted as a trust. “Lead trustee” means the trustee appointed by the trustees as lead trustee for registration on the database.

Who is making the entry

Draft regulation 4 says that, apart from listed cases, a landlord or dwelling entry may only be made by a person who is, or intends to become, a residential landlord, or by a person in one of the capacities in Schedule 2 paragraph 17. A letting agent is not in that list. Your agent cannot press submit for you. Regulation 4(5) separately allows a letting agent or property manager to provide the Part 2 tenancy and safety information.

Schedule 2 paragraphs 18 and 19. The draft regulations also ask for a certified or sealed copy of the authority. This sheet only records what you type about that document. It does not take the copy, and it does not decide that the capacity applies.

Dwelling information (Part 1)

The draft regulations say “ownership type” and do not list the options. The NRLA gives freehold and leasehold as examples. Write what applies, in your own words.

The draft regulations define dwelling type as the physical form, “such as a detached house, semi-detached house, terraced house or a flat”. Those are examples, not a closed list printed as a menu.

Name and email of any of the following. The draft regulations say “any of”, so a person who does not exist is not a missing document. Write “none” where there is nobody to name, so a blank is not mistaken for a finished answer.

Tenancy and health and safety (Part 2)

For a dwelling which is already let, the draft regulations allow the dwelling entry to be made first, and require this Part 2 information before the end of 28 days beginning with the day the entry was made. This page does not time that for you.

The draft regulations say “number of occupants”. They do not, in the text fetched, define occupants as excluding short-term guests. The NRLA describes this as tenants and permitted occupiers, not short-term guests. Use the NRLA gloss only as their description, not as a definition printed in the draft instrument.

The draft regulations ask for the number if available. This sheet does not tell you whether a licence is required. Holding a licence does not, on the NRLA’s account, take the place of a database entry.

If a gas safety record exists, the draft regulations ask for a copy as well as the issue date. This sheet does not take a copy. Keep the record and give it on the government service if you register.

Schedule 3 asks for a copy of the EICR, if one exists, or an appropriate EIC, and for the EICR expiry date or the EIC issue date. “Expiry” is the word used for the EICR, not for the EIC. No file is uploaded here.

Where the answer is yes, the draft regulations ask for a copy of the most recent EPC, if one exists. This sheet does not take that copy.

The draft regulations define “valid” by reference to regulation 9(2) of the Energy Performance of Buildings (England and Wales) Regulations 2012. This sheet does not apply that test.

Asked by the draft regulations if the most recent EPC is no longer valid. The NRLA also says that where an EPC was not required, or has lapsed since the tenancy began, the landlord must provide the tenancy start date.

The draft regulations point “minimum level of energy efficiency” to regulation 22(b) of the Energy Efficiency (Private Rented Property) (England and Wales) Regulations 2015. They do not print a letter or number for that minimum in the text fetched on 4 October 2026. This sheet will not guess it.

“Registered MEES exemption” in the draft regulations means an exemption relied on and registered on the PRS Exemptions Register. Writing it here does not register an exemption.
