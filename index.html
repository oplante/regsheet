RegSheet

A fill-in sheet of the fields the new “Register your rental property” service will ask for, so a landlord can copy them onto GOV.UK themselves.

Written for a landlord in England who manages the property themselves, starting with the West Midlands, because that is the first region named in the draft regulations. One payment for the sheet. No subscription. The sheet is a static form: what you type is placed on the page. Nothing is drafted for you by a model, and nothing is sent off this browser.

What you are buying, and what you are not

  • A blank sheet of the landlord entry and the dwelling entry, taken from Schedule 2 and Schedule 3 of the draft regulations, so you can gather the answers before the government service asks for them.
  • A print or a “save as PDF” from your own browser. Empty lines stay empty and are marked “you still need this”.
  • Not a registration, not a filing, and not a check that an answer is right.
  • Not the government fee. That is paid on GOV.UK, not to RegSheet.

When each region’s rules come into force

Registration is described as opening on 15 December 2026. The NRLA says that is the date the service opens for landlords in England, and that the requirement applies first in the West Midlands. The same page says a landlord may register a property from 15 December 2026 even if that region’s rules are not in force yet. LandlordZONE also says landlords can register before they are required to, and that the deadline follows the property’s location, not where the landlord lives.

The draft regulations themselves do not print a “deadline” column. Regulation 1 brings regulation 1 and regulation 3 into force on 15 December 2026. The rest come into force for entries about landlords and dwellings in each region on the dates below. For all other purposes, the remaining regulations come into force on 15 September 2027. That last date is in the instrument. It is not a tenth region.

The government fee is not our fee

The NRLA and LandlordZONE both say the charge is £65 per property per year, paid again on renewal. You pay that on the government service, not to RegSheet. The NRLA says the renewal date for a portfolio is the anniversary of the first property registered, and that a property registered later pays a pro-rata amount in the first year. The draft regulations say a fee must be paid before a dwelling entry is made or renewed, and that a later dwelling entry takes the same renewal date as an existing one. They do not print £65, and they do not print the pro-rata sentence. Regulation 14 says the database operator sets the amount by reference to the relevant costs. If the figure on GOV.UK is different when you pay, the government’s figure is the one that applies. This page cannot know it in advance.

Your agent cannot press submit for you

The draft regulations say a landlord entry and a dwelling entry are made in writing, on the database operator’s website or on its form. Except for a closed list of other people, only a person who is, or intends to become, a residential landlord may make the entry. The list covers capacities such as an attorney, a personal representative, a court appointed deputy, a trustee in bankruptcy, or a mortgagee in the circumstances the regulation describes. A letting agent is not on that list.

The NRLA says section 4 means the landlord must register unless an exception applies, and that agents are not an exception, so they cannot register on the landlord’s behalf. It also says agents may help by providing some information, and that government guidance for agents was still to be published. LandlordZONE says that if you use a letting agent you still need to start the registration yourself, and that there will be an option for the agent to upload certain information.

The draft regulations do say a letting agent or property manager may provide the Part 2 tenancy and safety information on behalf of the person making the dwelling entry. That is not the same as making the entry. This site does not submit anything for you, and it does not link to a payment or registration portal. The NRLA says the portal link is to be published closer to 15 December 2026.

Rent inclusive of utilities: no. Let unfurnished. Name on the landlord entry: A. Example. Date of birth: left blank.

On a printed sheet, the blank date of birth would read “you still need this”. The other lines would show only the words above. RegSheet would not add a reference number, a verdict, or a missing certificate.

What the NRLA says can follow from not registering

The NRLA’s guide, which it says is based on the legislation and on government statements, describes civil penalties under the Renters’ Rights Act: up to £7,000 for the section 82 matters it lists (marketing without an active entry, leaving the reference numbers off adverts, and failing to update a record), and up to £40,000 where section 92 applies (false or misleading information, or carrying on after a penalty or conviction in the way that section describes). It also describes rent repayment orders, a bar on a possession order in some cases, and powers of entry. The same guide says further legislation will be needed before local authorities use these enforcement powers.

Those amounts are not in the draft database regulations fetched for this site. This page does not tell you whether any of them would apply to a given property, and it does not tell you that filling in a sheet changes that.

Which properties the sources talk about

The NRLA says that, at the start, a property needs an entry if it is let under an assured or regulated tenancy, and that an empty property is not registered until it is occupied on an assured tenancy. It says a later duty, to register before marketing, needs further legislation and has no date. LandlordZONE says the same thing in shorter form: register if the property is currently let or becomes let during the rollout, and that unoccupied properties marketed for an assured tenancy come later, with the public interface.

The draft regulations use the Act’s words “residential landlord” and “residential tenancy”, and they were not fetched from the Act itself on this pass, so this site does not restate a definition the instrument only cross-refers. Regulation 3 would take a tenancy of supported exempt accommodation, as defined in the Supported Housing (Regulatory Oversight) Act 2023, out of “relevant tenancy” for this chapter. The draft also describes a dwelling entry for a dwelling which is intended to be let, and what must be added within 28 days of a tenancy starting. Read beside the NRLA’s “not until it is occupied” sentence, that is a difference of emphasis. This site does not choose for you which sentence covers a particular empty property.
