# Sources checked for RegSheet

Checked on 4 October 2026. Where the pages disagree, the draft regulations are followed, then the NRLA, then LandlordZONE. Quoted text below is limited to dates and the field list used on the sheet. Wording on the HTML pages is a paraphrase except where a date or a field name is copied.

The instrument was marked, on legislation.gov.uk: “This is a draft item of legislation and has not yet been made as a UK Statutory Instrument.”

## 1. Draft Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026

URL: https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348286861

Fetched in full on 4 October 2026. Preferred source for dates that the instrument itself states, and for the field list.

### Dates printed in regulation 1

- Regulation 1 and regulation 3 come into force on 15 December 2026.
- Remaining regulations, for entries in respect of residential landlords and dwellings in a named region, on the date in the table.
- For all other purposes, on 15 September 2027.

| Region | Date of coming into force of regulations |
| --- | --- |
| West Midlands | 15th December 2026 |
| East of England | 15th January 2027 |
| East Midlands | 15th February 2027 |
| South East | 15th March 2027 |
| Yorkshire and Humber | 15th April 2027 |
| North West | 15th May 2027 |
| North East | 15th June 2027 |
| London | 15th July 2027 |
| South West | 15th August 2027 |

Schedule 1 names the councils whose areas make up each region, “as they existed on 15th December 2026”. Those names are copied onto dates.html.

The instrument does **not** print the NRLA’s “by midnight” deadlines (14 March 2027 and the later midnight dates). It does **not** print a fee of £65. Regulation 14: the amount of the fee “is to be determined by the database operator by reference to the relevant costs.”

### Who may make an entry (used for the agent sentence)

Regulation 4(2): except as provided in paragraphs (3) to (5), a landlord or dwelling entry may only be made by a person who is, or intends to become, a residential landlord, or a person within Schedule 2 paragraph 17(a) to (j).

Regulation 4(5): the information and documents in Part 2 of Schedule 3 may be provided on behalf of the person making a dwelling entry by a letting agent or property manager appointed by them.

Regulation 6(2): a dwelling entry for a dwelling which is let may be made without Part 2, but Part 2 must be provided before the end of 28 days beginning with the date the dwelling entry was made.

Regulation 6(5): a dwelling entry may not be made unless the relevant fee has been paid by the person making the entry.

### Field list used (Schedule 2 and Schedule 3)

Landlord, where the person is an individual:

1. Name.
2. Date of birth.
3. Residential address.
4. Telephone number.
5. Email address.

Landlord, where the person is not an individual:

6. Name of organisation, or description by which it is known.
7. Type of legal entity.
8. Address (which may not be a PO Box number).
9. Telephone number.
10. Email address.
11. Name, date of birth, email address and telephone number of the individual making the entry on behalf of the organisation.
12. Name of nominated contact within the organisation for the purposes of the database, and their email address and telephone number.
13. Companies House Registration number, if applicable.
14. Charity number, if applicable.
15. Where a Companies House registration number is not provided, the names, dates of birth and addresses of all directors, trustees, partners or members of the governing body of the organisation.
16. Where the organisation is constituted as a trust, the name, date of birth, address, email address and telephone number of the lead trustee.

In all cases:

17. Whether the person making the entry is doing so as one of the listed capacities: (a) donee of a power of attorney; (b) personal representative of a deceased person’s estate; (c) court appointed deputy; (d) guardian; (e) receiver; (f) trustee in bankruptcy; (g) liquidator or provisional liquidator; (h) administrator; (i) administrative receiver; (j) a person acting in a similar or corresponding capacity under the law of a country or territory outside England and Wales; (k) a mortgagee or a person appointed by the mortgagee.
18. The name, address, telephone number and email address of any person specified under paragraph 17.
19. A copy of a certified or sealed copy of the relevant evidence of authority. The sheet records a description and date only. It does not take the copy.

Dwelling, Part 1:

1. Address of dwelling.
2. Name of the person who is the residential landlord, or who intends to become the residential landlord, of the dwelling, and a correspondence address (which may not be a PO Box number) in England and Wales, and an email address for that person in relation to the dwelling.
3. Ownership type.
4. Dwelling type. The definition says the physical form, “such as a detached house, semi-detached house, terraced house or a flat”.
5. Number of bedrooms in the dwelling.
6. Whether the dwelling is currently under let.
7. Name and email address of any of: (a) the owner of the freehold property which includes the dwelling; (b) any other superior landlord; (c) the property manager for the dwelling.

Dwelling, Part 2:

8. Number of occupants of the dwelling.
9. Number of households occupying the dwelling.
10. Whether the dwelling requires an HMO licence, an additional licence or a selective licence, and if so, the numbers of those licences, if available.
11. If the dwelling has a gas supply: (a) a copy of the gas safety record, if one exists, and (b) the gas safety record issue date.
12. Electrical safety: (a) a copy of the EICR, if one exists, or appropriate EIC, and (b) the EICR expiry date, or the EIC issue date.
13. Whether the landlord was required to give a valid EPC to the person who is the tenant currently occupying the dwelling, and if so, a copy of the most recent EPC, if one exists.
14. If the most recent EPC is no longer valid, the date the current tenancy started.
15. If the most recent EPC gives an energy performance rating below the minimum level of energy efficiency, whether the dwelling has a registered MEES exemption, and if so, what types of exemption apply.
16. Rent charged and frequency of payment, and whether the rent is inclusive of utilities and if so, what utilities are included.
17. Whether the dwelling is let furnished, partly furnished or unfurnished.

Copies of the gas safety record, EICR or EIC, EPC, and authority document are named in the instrument. The sheet does not accept files. The printout says the copy is not stored.

## 2. NRLA — Register your rental property

URL: https://www.nrla.org.uk/resources/renters-rights/landlord-database-register-your-rental-property-service

Fetched 4 October 2026. The page says the Government published the Private Rented Sector Database Regulations 2026 on 9 September 2026. It does not show a clock time.

### Dates used from this page

- Service known as the “Register Your Rental Property Service”.
- Opens on 15 December 2026.
- West Midlands must register by 14 March 2027.
- Fee of £65 per annum for each property. Paid on renewal each year. The page says the renewal date for a portfolio is the date the first property was registered, and that a property registered later pays a pro-rata amount in the first year. The pro-rata sentence is **not** in the draft regulations, so the site attributes it to the NRLA only.
- Portal link “will be published closer to December 15th 2026”. No URL was on the page. None is invented here.

NRLA table, “Deadline to register (by midnight)”, used as the third column and not as a date in the draft instrument:

| Region | Date regulations commence | Deadline to register (by midnight) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| West Midlands | 15 December 2026 | 14 March 2027 |
| East of England | 15 January 2027 | 14 April 2027 |
| East Midlands | 15 February 2027 | 14 May 2027 |
| South East | 15 March 2027 | 14 June 2027 |
| Yorkshire and Humber | 15 April 2027 | 14 July 2027 |
| North West | 15 May 2027 | 14 August 2027 |
| North East | 15 June 2027 | 14 September 2027 |
| London | 15 July 2027 | 14 October 2027 |
| South West | 15 August 2027 | 14 November 2027 |

The “commence” column matches regulation 1. The midnight column is the NRLA’s. The page says landlords have three months to pay the fee and register once registration is required in the region, and that any property may be registered from 15 December 2026 even if that region’s regulations do not yet apply.

### Agent

“No, Section 4 of the Private Rented Sector Database Regulations explicitly states the landlord must register unless one of the exceptions apply. Agents are not listed as an exception to this rule, so cannot register on the landlord’s behalf.” The page adds that agents can help by providing some information, and that guidance for agents was still to come.

### NRLA field summary — not used where it differs

The sheet follows Schedule 2 and Schedule 3, not this summary. Differences noted so they are not silently merged:

- NRLA says “service address” of the landlord. The draft says a correspondence address in England and Wales, which may not be a PO Box.
- NRLA’s examples of ownership type are “freehold, leasehold, etc”. The draft says “ownership type” only. The sheet is a free-text line plus that note.
- NRLA says “part furnished”. The draft says “partly furnished”. The sheet uses “partly furnished”.
- NRLA describes occupants as “tenants and permitted occupiers but not short term guests”. The draft says “number of occupants” and does not add that gloss. The sheet asks for the number and shows the NRLA sentence as their description only.
- NRLA says the EICR or EIC “and the date it expires on”. The draft asks for the EICR expiry date **or** the EIC **issue** date. The sheet follows the draft.
- NRLA says rent “whether it includes bills”. The draft says utilities, and which utilities. The sheet follows the draft and mentions the NRLA’s shorter wording.

Penalty figures on the NRLA page (up to £7,000 under the section 82 matters it lists; up to £40,000 under the section 92 matters it lists) are repeated once on index.html, with the NRLA’s own sentence that further legislation is needed before councils use those powers. They are not in the draft database regulations. They are not in the site headline.

## 3. LandlordZONE

URL: https://www.landlordzone.co.uk/news/landlord-database-to-roll-out-in-december-with-ps65-fee

Fetched 4 October 2026. Byline Helen Gregory. The page shows “September 9, 2026 11:51 AM”. No time zone is printed on the fetched page.

### Dates and fee used from this page

- West Midlands: registering from 15 December.
- Then East of England (15 January), East Midlands (15 February), South East (15 March), Yorkshire and Humber (15 April), North West (15 May), North East (15 June), London (15 July), South West (15 August).
- “Landlords have a three-month deadline to register before local councils can start enforcement activity.” The midnight dates are not printed here.
- Annual £65 per property, to renew each year.
- Deadline follows the property’s location. Example given: a landlord living in London with a property in Birmingham uses the West Midlands deadline.
- Landlords can register before being required to.
- “If you use a letting agent, you will still need to start the registration process yourself. There will be an option for your agent to upload certain information on your behalf.”

Fields mentioned, all of which also appear in the draft list: date of birth, address, phone number, email address, information about rent, address, number of residents and bedrooms, whether an HMO, additional, or selective licence is needed, and gas, electric, and EPC. The page says a full list is “available here” but the fetched article did not include that list. The sheet does not take fields from this article that are not in the draft schedules.

## Not verified, and not asserted

- A live GOV.UK registration URL. The NRLA said it was not published yet.
- The numeral £65 inside the draft instrument. It is in the NRLA and LandlordZONE pieces only.
- The midnight deadlines inside the draft instrument. They are in the NRLA table only. LandlordZONE supports a three-month period but does not print those end dates.
- The letter or number of the “minimum level of energy efficiency”. The draft points to regulation 22(b) of the Energy Efficiency (Private Rented Property) (England and Wales) Regulations 2015 and does not quote a band.
- The Act’s own definition of “residential tenancy” and “residential landlord” (sections 63 and 75 of the Renters’ Rights Act 2025). The draft cross-refers to them. The NRLA describes them as assured or regulated tenancies. The Act text was not fetched, so the site does not treat that description as the instrument’s wording.
- Whether an empty property must be registered before it is let. The NRLA and LandlordZONE say not yet. The draft also provides for an entry for a dwelling intended to be let. The site states both and does not pick.
- Any closed list of ownership types, legal-entity types, or rent frequencies. Not in the fetched instrument.
- That the draft has been approved or brought into force. The page said it had not yet been made.
